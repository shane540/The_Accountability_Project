Federal Judges Impeached Ever
⚠ FEDERAL JUDGES CANNOT BE RECALLED. They serve during "good behavior" under Article III. The ONLY constitutional removal mechanism is impeachment by the House and conviction by 2/3 of the Senate. However, misconduct short of removal can be addressed through the Judicial Conduct and Disability Act process — and criminal conduct can be prosecuted while a judge remains on the bench.

FEDERAL JUDICIAL ACCOUNTABILITY TOOLS

Judicial Conduct Complaint
U.S. Courts — Judicial Council
File misconduct complaints against any federal judge under the Judicial Conduct and Disability Act. Each circuit has a Judicial Council that receives and investigates complaints. Can result in censure, referral to the Special Committee, or referral to the Judicial Conference for impeachment.

Impeachment — House Judiciary
U.S. House of Representatives
The only removal mechanism for federal judges. House Judiciary Committee investigates and recommends. Full House votes to impeach (simple majority). Senate holds trial (2/3 to convict). 15 federal judges have been impeached — 8 convicted and removed.
CONTACT →House Judiciary Committeejudiciary.house.gov DEMAND →Contact Your Representativehouse.gov/find-rep HISTORY →Federal Impeachment Historyhistory.house.gov
DOJ / FBI Criminal Referral
Department of Justice
Judges are not immune from criminal prosecution for conduct outside their judicial duties. Bribery of a federal judge is a federal felony. Report to DOJ Public Integrity Section or FBI. A criminal conviction typically triggers impeachment proceedings.
REPORT →DOJ Public Integrityjustice.gov/criminal/pis TIP →FBI Tip Linetips.fbi.gov OIG →DOJ Inspector Generaloig.justice.gov
Recusal Motion
28 U.S.C. § 455
Any party in a case can file a motion demanding a judge recuse themselves for bias, conflict of interest, or appearance of impropriety. File in the case record. If denied, appeal to the circuit. Documented recusal refusals strengthen future misconduct complaints.
LAW →28 U.S.C. § 455 — Recusallaw.cornell.edu FILE →Follow With Conduct Complaintuscourts.gov
Supreme Court Ethics Complaint
U.S. Supreme Court (NEW — 2023)
The Supreme Court adopted a Code of Conduct in November 2023 — the last court in the country to do so. Complaints about Supreme Court justices can now be filed under this code. Enforcement mechanism is still developing but sets a public accountability standard.
CODE →SCOTUS Code of Conductsupremecourt.gov SENATE →Senate Judiciary Committeejudiciary.senate.gov LAW →Supreme Court Ethics Actcongress.gov

WHAT JUDICIAL CONDUCT COMMISSIONS CANNOT DO

Conduct commissions cannot overturn a judge's legal rulings — even bad ones. Disagreement with a legal decision is not misconduct. The correct remedy for wrong rulings is appeal. Commissions handle conduct — how a judge behaves — not legal conclusions — what a judge decides. If you believe a ruling was legally wrong: appeal. If you believe a judge behaved improperly in reaching it: commission complaint.

HOW TO FILE A JUDICIAL CONDUCT COMPLAINT — STEP BY STEP

1
Identify the correct body. Federal judge → file with the Judicial Council of the circuit where the judge sits (uscourts.gov). State judge → file with your state's judicial conduct commission (find yours in the All 50 States tab). Wrong body = complaint dismissed without review.
2
Document specific conduct — not rulings. Commission complaints must allege specific misconduct — what the judge did, when, where, and who witnessed it. "I disagree with the ruling" is not grounds. "The judge had an undisclosed financial relationship with a party in the case" is grounds. Be specific, be factual, include dates.
3
Gather supporting documentation. Court transcripts, financial disclosure records, public records, witness statements, news reports, official filings. Every claim should have a document attached. Unsupported allegations are easier to dismiss.
4
File in writing — use their form. Every commission has a standard complaint form. Use it. Unformatted complaints get deprioritized. Most commissions accept complaints by mail, some accept online. Keep a copy of everything you submit with a delivery confirmation.
5
Understand confidentiality. Most judicial conduct proceedings are confidential until a formal charge is filed. This means your complaint may be dismissed without public notice. Request written acknowledgment of receipt and written notice of any dismissal with the reason stated.
6
File simultaneously with multiple bodies. File the conduct commission complaint AND a recusal motion (if still in the case) AND a DOJ referral (if criminal conduct) on the same day. For state judges also file with the state AG if crimes are involved. Multiple simultaneous filings are harder to bury.
7
Make it public — after filing. After submitting, publish everything. Issue a press statement. Contact local journalists. The complaint itself is your documentation. "I have filed a formal judicial conduct complaint with the [Circuit/State] alleging..." — that sentence creates accountability the complaint alone may not.
8
For state judges — organize a retention campaign. If the judge faces a retention election, an organized opposition campaign is the most powerful tool available. The Iowa 2010 example: three Iowa Supreme Court justices were removed by voters after a coordinated campaign opposing their retention. No litigation required — just votes.
THE IOWA 2010 PRECEDENT

Three Iowa Supreme Court justices who ruled in favor of same-sex marriage faced a coordinated retention election campaign in 2010. All three were removed by voters — the first time Iowa had ever voted out a Supreme Court justice. This proved that organized retention campaigns can remove judges that conduct commissions and impeachment processes protect. If your target judge faces a retention election: organize, fund, and run the campaign.
